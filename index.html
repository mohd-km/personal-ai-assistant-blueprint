A blueprint for a personal AI assistant whose memory lives in one folder of plain text notes that you own. Three different AI apps (Antigravity, Claude and Codex) all read the same notes, so they all know your preferences, and if one stops working the next one carries on.

PART 1 — FOLLOW ONE REQUEST

The journey of a request, in five stops

Laptop → shared folder → cloud computer → your phone → your OK. Click any stop on the diagram to see what happens there.

The problemYou ask an assistant to do something, but you cannot see where your words go or what stops it from sending an email you did not mean to send.

How it worksEach request (shown as a moving dot, called a packet) travels five stops. Your laptop finds the right notes, the shared folder copies them to an always-on cloud computer, an AI app there does the work, the result is sent to your phone on Telegram, and a final safety check (the approval gate) holds anything that would leave your hands until you agree.

Try this firstPress Send a client email. The dot stops at the last box and shows you the draft email with Approve & Send and Cancel Send. Then try Remember travel preference: it passes straight through, because a private note needs no permission.

PART 2 — MAKE YOUR OWN

Build your starter kit

Pick your options and download three ready-made files. Nothing is uploaded: the files are made inside your browser.

The problemSetting up an assistant like this from scratch means writing the rules and settings yourself, and most people do not know where to begin.

How it worksYour choices fill in three files. AGENTS.md is the instruction sheet every AI app reads first. config.json is a short settings file listing your apps, folder and phone channel. memory/main.md is the index page of your notes.

PART 3 — WHEN AN AI GOES DOWN

Switch an AI off

The backup plan is called failover: when one AI app is unavailable, the next one in line takes over.

The problemAI apps have outages and usage limits. If your assistant only works with one of them, it simply stops.

How it worksThe three apps are lined up in order. Work goes to the first one that is switched on. Because all three read the same notes, the backup already knows everything and nothing has to be copied or re-explained. Each runs on a fixed monthly plan, so a switch adds no pay-per-use charges.

Try this firstSwitch Antigravity to Offline. Claude moves to ACTIVE NOW, and the diagram in Part 1 shows Claude doing the work.

All three assistants are switched off. Your notes stay safe in the folder, and nothing is charged, until any of them comes back.

Why this avoids surprise bills

Because every assistant reads the same folder of notes, moving from Antigravity to Claude or Codex needs no copying and no repeating yourself. Many AI services also sell pay-per-use access (an API, where a program talks to the AI directly and is billed per word). This design only uses fixed monthly plans, so the extra pay-per-use cost stays at $0 even during an outage.

PART 4 — WHAT IS IN THE MEMORY

Inside the 5 Folders

The whole memory is five ordinary folders of text files.

The problemChat apps bury what you told them inside long conversation histories that you cannot easily search, fix or take with you.

How it worksEach folder holds one kind of knowledge: rules for how to behave, facts about you, past mistakes to avoid, step-by-step recipes, and a log of everything done. They are written in Markdown, which is just plain text with a few symbols such as # for a heading and - for a bullet point.

Try this firstClick Lessons Learned. You will see a sample note: one correction written here is followed by all three AI apps from then on.
